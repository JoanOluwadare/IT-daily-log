# Notes
- For a typical multirotor, the landing process may involve approching the designated location, descending at a controlled rate, touching down on the landing surface and disarming the motors. The precise sequence depends on the drone and its flight-control system. 
- Battery capacity refers to the amount of energy the battery can store. 
- State of charge refers to how much energy remains, relative to the battery's capacity. 
- Flight time refers to the duration of flight and is related to battery. 
- Endurance is how long an aircraft can remain airborne, under specific conditions. 
Some flight-control systems can trigger a return or landing action at configured battery thresholds. The exact behaviour depends on the system configuration. 
- Telemetry allows the remote pilot to recieve information about the aircraft in operation. 
- Telemetry can transmit information on aircraft position, altitude, speed, battery voltage and remaining charge, GNSS status, warnings and system health information.
- Telemtry is transmitted through the C2 link. 
- It is not the same as flight log. It is data transmitted for real-time monitoring, while the flight log is for later review and analysis. 
- A waypoint is a specified reference position used as a refernce point in a possible route. The route is the planned series of locations the aircraft should follow through space. The flight path is the path it actually follows during flight, affected by obstacles, wind and other factors. The mission plan is the broader set of instructions and objectives for the flight, potentially including waypoints, actions and landing instructions. 
- None of these automatically guarantee that the flight would be conducted safely. The remote pilot still needs to understand the aircraft, follow its procedures and account for the operating environment.
